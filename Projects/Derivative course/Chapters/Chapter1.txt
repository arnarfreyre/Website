## 1. Introduction and Basic Concepts {#introduction}

### What Are Derivatives?
A derivative is a financial instrument that derives its value from the performance of an underlying entity. The most commonly traded derivatives include:
- **Forwards/Futures**: Agreements to deliver or receive assets at a future date for a price set today
- **Options**: Contracts offering the right (but not obligation) to buy or sell an asset
- **Swaps**: Agreements to exchange fixed against variable cashflows

### Key Market Concepts

#### Bid-Ask Spread
- **Bid**: The price a buyer is willing to pay for an asset
- **Ask**: The price a seller is willing to accept for an asset
- **Spread**: ASK - BID = SPREAD

The spread reflects liquidity, transaction costs, and market efficiency. Narrow spreads indicate highly liquid markets, while wide spreads suggest information asymmetry or low liquidity.

### Primary Uses of Derivatives
1. **Hedging (Risk Mitigation)**: Protecting against adverse price movements
2. **Speculation**: Taking leveraged positions based on market views

## 2. Forward Contracts Fundamentals {#forward-fundamentals}

### Definition
A forward contract is an agreement between two parties to buy or sell an asset at a specified future time (maturity T) for a price fixed today (delivery price F).

### Key Characteristics
- **Zero initial value**: No cash exchange when entering the contract
- **Settlement at maturity**: Payment and delivery occur at time T
- **Over-the-counter (OTC)**: Customized bilateral agreements
- **Payoff structure**:
  - Long position payoff: V(t,T) = S(T) - F(t,T)
  - Short position payoff: V(t,T) = F(t,T) - S(T)

### Forward vs. Futures
**Essential Difference**:
- **Forwards**: Settled only at maturity, traded OTC
- **Futures**: Settled daily (mark-to-market), traded on exchanges

## 3. Fair Pricing of Forward Contracts {#fair-pricing}

### Basic Pricing Formula (No Dividends)
For an asset with no dividends, storage costs, or other benefits:

**Forward Price**: F(t,T) = S(t) × e^(R(t,T) × (T-t))

Where:
- S(t) = Current spot price
- R(t,T) = Risk-free interest rate
- T-t = Time to maturity

### Arbitrage-Free Pricing Logic

#### Step-by-Step Demonstration:
1. **Assume**: F(t,T) > S(t) × e^(R(T-t) × (T-t))
2. **Arbitrage Strategy**:
   - Short the forward contract
   - Borrow S(t) and buy the asset
   - At maturity T: Deliver asset, receive F(t,T), repay loan
3. **Risk-free Profit**: F(t,T) - S(t) × e^(R(T-t) × (T-t)) > 0

This arbitrage opportunity would be eliminated by market forces, establishing the fair price.

### Four Pay-Delivery Alternatives

| Strategy | Time of Payment | Time of Receipt | Amount of Payment |
|----------|----------------|-----------------|-------------------|
| Outright purchase | 0 | 0 | S(0) |
| Fully leveraged purchase | T | 0 | S(0)e^(rT) |
| Prepaid forward contract | 0 | T | F₀,T^P |
| Forward contract | T | T | F₀,T |

## 4. Advanced Pricing Considerations {#advanced-pricing}

### Forward Pricing with Discrete Dividends
For an asset paying dividend Cc at time Tc < T:

**Formula**: F(t,T) = [S(t) - PV(Cc)] × e^(R(t,T) × (T-t))

Where PV(Cc) = Cc × e^(-R(t,Tc) × (Tc-t))

### Forward Pricing with Continuous Dividends
For assets with continuous dividend yield δ:

**Formula**: F(t,T) = S(t) × e^((R(t,T) - δ) × (T-t))

### Step-by-Step Calculation Example

**Given**:
- Spot price S(0) = $100
- Risk-free rate r = 5%
- Dividend yield δ = 2%
- Time to maturity T = 0.5 years

**Solution**:
1. Calculate the exponent: (r - δ) × T = (0.05 - 0.02) × 0.5 = 0.015
2. Calculate e^0.015 = 1.0151
3. Forward price: F(0,0.5) = $100 × 1.0151 = $101.51

## 5. Market Conditions: Contango and Backwardation {#market-conditions}

### Forward Premium
The ratio between forward and spot prices:
**FP(t,T) = F(t,T) / S(t) = e^((R(t,T) - δ) × (T-t))**

### Market Structures

#### Contango
- **Definition**: Forward/futures price > Expected spot price
- **Characteristics**:
  - Forward premium > 1 (for non-dividend assets)
  - Common in commodities with storage costs
  - Contract value converges down to spot at maturity

#### Backwardation
- **Definition**: Forward/futures price < Expected spot price
- **Characteristics**:
  - Forward premium < 1
  - Often occurs with high convenience yield
  - Contract value converges up to spot at maturity

### Trading Implications

**Contango Trading Strategy**:
- Short near-month contract
- Long far-month contract
- Profits if contango structure increases

**Backwardation Trading Strategy**:
- Long futures hoping for convergence to higher spot
- Profitable if commodity price trends higher

## 6. Practical Applications and Examples {#practical-applications}

### Example 1: Currency Hedging

**Scenario**: US company needs £1 million in 90 days

**Given**:
- Spot rate: 1.3645 USD/GBP
- 90-day forward: 1.3798 USD/GBP

**Analysis**:
- If rate rises to 1.4000: Gain = (1.4000 - 1.3798) × 1M = $20,200
- If rate falls to 1.3450: Loss = (1.3450 - 1.3798) × 1M = -$34,800

**Decision**: Forward contract eliminates exchange rate risk

### Example 2: Commodity Hedging (Wheat Farmer)

**Strategy**:
1. Estimate harvest quantity
2. Short sell wheat futures for that amount
3. Results:
   - If spot price falls → Short position profits offset lower sales price
   - If spot price rises → Short position losses offset by higher sales price
4. **Outcome**: Total revenue becomes predictable

### Key Takeaways for Practitioners

1. **Always Check for Arbitrage**: Ensure pricing aligns with theoretical models
2. **Consider All Costs**: Include dividends, storage, and convenience yield
3. **Monitor Market Structure**: Contango/backwardation affects roll strategies
4. **Understand Settlement**: Forward (at maturity) vs. Futures (daily)
5. **Risk Management**: Use forwards/futures to hedge, not just speculate

### Calculator Quick Reference

**Basic Forward Price**:
```
F = S × e^(r×T)
```

**With Dividends**:
```
F = S × e^((r-q)×T)
```

**With Storage Costs (u)**:
```
F = S × e^((r+u-y)×T)
```
Where y = convenience yield

## Important Notes and Warnings

⚠️ **Credit Risk**: Forward contracts carry counterparty risk (unlike exchange-traded futures)

⚠️ **Liquidity**: OTC forwards may have limited secondary market liquidity

⚠️ **Margin Requirements**: Futures require initial margin; forwards typically don't

⚠️ **Market Conditions**: Contango/backwardation can flip rapidly - strategies based on single conditions may become unprofitable
