1. Time Value of Money

Compound Interest (Discrete)

$$FV = PV \times (1 + r)^n$$

Variables:

  • $FV$ = Future Value
  • $PV$ = Present Value
  • $r$ = Interest rate per period
  • $n$ = Number of periods

For finding periods: $n = \frac{\ln(FV/PV)}{\ln(1+r)}$

Present Value of Single Cash Flow

$$PV = \frac{CF}{(1 + r)^t}$$

Variables:

  • $CF$ = Cash flow at time $t$
  • $r$ = Discount rate
  • $t$ = Time period

Annuity Present Value

$$PV = C \times \frac{1 - (1 + r)^{-n}}{r}$$

Variables:

  • $C$ = Regular payment amount
  • $r$ = Interest rate per period
  • $n$ = Number of payments

Used for bonds with regular coupon payments

2. Bond Pricing

Bond Price with Semi-Annual Coupons

$$P = \sum_{t=1}^{n} \frac{C/2}{(1 + r/2)^t} + \frac{F}{(1 + r/2)^n}$$

Variables:

  • $P$ = Bond price
  • $C$ = Annual coupon payment
  • $r$ = Annual yield (with semi-annual compounding)
  • $n$ = Number of semi-annual periods
  • $F$ = Face value (par value)

† Important: For bonds with semi-annual coupons, use semi-annual compounding!

Bond Price with Varying Term Structure

$$P = \sum_{i=1}^{n} \frac{CF_i}{(1 + r_i/2)^{2t_i}}$$

Where:

  • $CF_i$ = Cash flow at period $i$
  • $r_i$ = Annual spot rate for period $i$
  • $t_i$ = Time in years to period $i$

Each cash flow is discounted at its corresponding spot rate

Clean vs. Dirty Price

$$\text{Dirty Price} = \text{Clean Price} + \text{Accrued Interest}$$
$$\text{Accrued Interest} = C \times \frac{\text{Days since last coupon}}{\text{Days in coupon period}}$$

3. Duration Measures

Effective Duration

$$D_{eff} = \frac{PV_{-} - PV_{+}}{2 \times PV_0 \times \Delta r}$$

Variables:

  • $PV_{-}$ = Bond price when rate decreases by $\Delta r$
  • $PV_{+}$ = Bond price when rate increases by $\Delta r$
  • $PV_0$ = Initial bond price
  • $\Delta r$ = Change in interest rate (typically 0.0001 or 1 basis point)

Measures price sensitivity to interest rate changes

Macaulay Duration

$$D_{Mac} = \frac{\sum_{t=1}^{n} t \times \frac{CF_t}{(1+r)^t}}{P}$$

Variables:

  • $t$ = Time to cash flow (in years)
  • $CF_t$ = Cash flow at time $t$
  • $r$ = Yield per period
  • $P$ = Current bond price

Weighted average time to receive cash flows

Modified Duration

$$D_{Mod} = \frac{D_{Mac}}{1 + r/m}$$

Variables:

  • $D_{Mac}$ = Macaulay duration
  • $r$ = Annual yield
  • $m$ = Compounding frequency per year

For semi-annual bonds: $m = 2$

Duration-Based Price Change Approximation

$$\frac{\Delta P}{P} \approx -D_{Mod} \times \Delta y$$

Where:

  • $\Delta P$ = Change in bond price
  • $P$ = Initial bond price
  • $D_{Mod}$ = Modified duration
  • $\Delta y$ = Change in yield

4. Risk Metrics

Expected Credit Loss (ECL)

$$ECL = EAD \times PD \times LGD$$

Variables:

  • $EAD$ = Exposure at Default (amount at risk)
  • $PD$ = Probability of Default
  • $LGD$ = Loss Given Default (percentage of EAD lost)

Example: EAD = $8B, PD = 2.3%, LGD = 60% í ECL = $110.4M

Convexity

$$C = \frac{1}{P} \times \frac{\partial^2 P}{\partial y^2}$$

Measures the curvature of price-yield relationship

Value at Risk (VaR)

$$VaR_{\alpha} = -\inf\{x : P(L > x) \leq 1 - \alpha\}$$

Where:

  • $L$ = Loss random variable
  • $\alpha$ = Confidence level (e.g., 95%, 99%)

5. Interest Rate Structures

Term Structure Interpolation

$$r(t) = r_0 + \text{slope} \times (t - t_0)$$

Example from Problem Set 1:

  • For $t \leq 7$ years: $r(t) = 4\% + 0.000352 \times (t - 0.5) \times 2$
  • For $t > 7$ years: $r(t) = 4.4576\%$ (constant)

Forward Rate

$$f(t_1, t_2) = \frac{(1 + r_2)^{t_2}}{(1 + r_1)^{t_1}} - 1$$

Where:

  • $f(t_1, t_2)$ = Forward rate from $t_1$ to $t_2$
  • $r_1, r_2$ = Spot rates for periods $t_1$ and $t_2$

Yield to Maturity (YTM)

$$P = \sum_{t=1}^{n} \frac{C}{(1 + YTM)^t} + \frac{F}{(1 + YTM)^n}$$

YTM is the rate that equates bond price to present value of cash flows

Solve iteratively (no closed-form solution)

Important Notes & Common Mistakes

† Critical Reminders

  • Semi-annual bonds: Always use semi-annual compounding $(1 + r/2)^{2t}$
  • Duration: Should typically be less than maturity for coupon bonds
  • Basis points: 1 basis point = 0.01% = 0.0001
  • Time vs. Periods: Distinguish between years $(t)$ and periods $(n = 2t$ for semi-annual)
  • Python verification: Every calculation must be verified before use!
