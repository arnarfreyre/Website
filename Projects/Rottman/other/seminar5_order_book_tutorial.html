Understanding the Order Book (Tilboðsbókin)

What is an Order Book?

The order book (Tilboðsbókin) is a list of trading orders in an exchange that shows the interest of buyers and sellers for a specific financial instrument. It's the heart of modern electronic markets!

Key Terms

  • Bid (Boð) - Buy order: The price buyers are willing to pay
  • Ask/Offer (Tilboð) - Sell order: The price sellers are willing to accept
  • Spread (Verðbil): The difference between the best bid and best ask
  • Depth (Dýpt): The quantity available at each price level

💡 Example from Seminar

XYZ Stock: 50.10 bid for 1000 shares | 500 shares offered at 50.20

This means: Buyers want to buy 1000 shares at 50.10, Sellers want to sell 500 shares at 50.20

The spread is: 50.20 - 50.10 = 0.10

Order Types & Conditions

1. Limit Order (Takmarkstilboð)

Sets a maximum buy price or minimum sell price. The order will only execute at this price or better.

Example: "Buy 100 XYZ at 50.20" - Will buy at 50.20 or lower

2. Market Order (Markaðstilboð)

Executes immediately at the best available price. No price limit specified.

Example: "Buy 100 XYZ at market" - Takes the best ask price immediately

Special Order Conditions

IOC (Immediate or Cancel)

Execute now or cancel. Partial fills allowed.

AON (All or None)

Fill completely or not at all. No partial fills.

FOK (Fill or Kill)

IOC + AON: Fill completely now or cancel.

Order Duration

  • Day Order: Valid only for the current trading day
  • GTC (Good Till Cancelled): Remains active until filled or manually cancelled
💡 Remember: IOC, AON, and FOK orders are NOT added to the order book if they can't be executed immediately!

How Orders Match

Immediate Execution Terms

  • "Hit the Bid" (Slá biddið): To sell immediately at the bid price
  • "Lift the Ask" (Fara á askið): To buy immediately at the ask price
